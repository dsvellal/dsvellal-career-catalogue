1.5 Delimitations of the Study
The nature of any laboratory experiment warrants a boundary to be placed on the
scope of the research study or else it is difficult to draw conclusions. The measure of
the effect of pranayama on software engineer’s stress level is without exception. The
following are the delimitations of the study:
 This study is limited to the measurement of the effect of a corporate
pranayama program on the Stress levels of employees working for IBM.
 Restricted to the age group of 25 – 40.
 Employees working for IBM Bangalore employees are considered
 The duration of the current study has been time boxed at six weeks of
classroom instruction. Pranayama is very subtle practice and in many cases
requires a settle in period for the practitioner to start reaping the benefits.
 The experimental group comprises of software engineers of belonging to the
same organization.
 The stress level is measured by the Questionnaire provided by the Human
Resource of the company.

5 Findings, Conclusion and Suggestion for future research
This study may be interpreted as demonstrating evidence of the relation between
Pranayama practice and the benefits thereof for SWE’s related to stress at work. As
evidenced in previous work, (Frew, D., 1974) gains in job satisfaction and
performance as well as interpersonal relations were significantly more.

5.1 Summary
These findings were taken into account during the design of this study, especially at
defining the criteria regarding participant profiles, and are one of the probable
reasons why evidence on benefits gained by the Pranayama practice group seems to
be fairly strong after results measured on variable submitted to research. By
evidence gathered along this study, it is quite clear that the introduction of a
structured set of Pranayama techniques deliver the above mentioned benefits to
SWE’s productivity at work.

5.2 Findings
Based on the Hypothesis formulated for this study we can summarize the following
findings. The Hypotheses H, states that “there will be reduction in the level of
stress among the corporate employees working for IBM after the practice of
Pranayama”. This has been proved to be true based on the data evidence obtained
from collating responses from participants to the stress questionnaire that was
given to the SWE’s both before and after conducting the Pranayama program. It
was found that among the highly stress employees working for IBM, there was an
improvement of 63.54% with a correlation of -3.99 and among the moderately
40

stressed employees, there was an improvement of 45.13% in the stress levels with a
correlation of 0.12, after the practice of Sectional breathing and Bhramari
pranayama. Scores were computed based on the responses and overall status
determined by the total scored by each subject. While the results are encouraging to
the study, the stress data collected was more qualitative in nature and may be more
relevant if computed by an empirical or quantitative means.

5.3 Conclusion
We have seen empirical evidence of positive benefits from Pranayama, but as
described at various points in this study, the duration of the course was too short to
see significant benefits. One of the most significant learning is the need for a longer
and more consistent Pranayama program preferably corporate sponsored so that
the employees can avail of this at the workplace and in turn the project/company
can gain significant revenue. The other main worry from the standpoint of the study
was the fact that work pressures/business travel could affect the results of the study
as the researcher did not have control over these parameters. This however did not
seem to have a very great impact on the overall data and result.

5.4 Suggestion for future research
Based on the learning from this study and the limitation stated above, it is
important to take up future research in this area where the experimental group
comprises of a cross section of different roles in the company to study the possibility
of generalization.

7.1.1

Vibhagha Pranayama or Sectional Breathing

This is a preparatory breathing practice for Pranayama that corrects the breathing
pattern and helps to increase lung capacity by encouraging fuller breathing into the
lungs. It has a deeply calming effect on body and mind. It is used as an introduction
to the Full Yogic Breath. There are three sections of Sectional Breathing:
7.1.1.1 Mehod of Sectional breathing
7.1.1.1.1 Abdominal Breathing

Also known as Diaphragmatic Breathing, it is breathing into the lower region of the
lung. For practicing this breathing sit erect in Vajrasana with fingers on either side
of your navel, with elbows resting at your sides. First exhale completely, slowly and
continuously. This exhalation is known as Puraka. Now take three breaths into
focused area and feel your abdomen rising and falling beneath your hands. Stop the
breath for a second in this position and then exhale. While exhaling the abdomen
should be drawn inwards continuously and slowly. Before reversing the breath, stop
the breath for a second and inhale. Repeat the breathing cycle smoothly.
7.1.1.1.2 Thoracic Breathing

Also called Intercostal breathing this is the breathing into the middle region of the
lung. For this breathing technique sit in Vajrasana and place your hands on each
side of your rib cage. Take three breaths, feeling your rib cage expanding sideway
under your hands as you inhale and relaxing as you exhale. In this breathing the
49

air should be filled in chest not in abdomen, hence only chest should be expanded
and contracted and abdomen should be controlled to avoid its bulging.
7.1.1.1.3 Clavicular Breathing

Known as Upper lobar breathing also it is breathing into the upper region of the
lung. For practicing this breathing sit in Vajrasana and place your fingers
underneath your collarbones. As you inhale, feel your upper chest rising slightly.
Keep your shoulders relaxed; avoid raising or tension them. In this breathing the
air is forced into the uppermost regions of the lungs thus ventilating the upper
lobes.
7.1.1.1.4 Full Yogic Breathing

A full Yogic breath combines all three breathing techniques of Sectional breathing.
It starts with Abdominal breathing and continued with the thoracic and clavicular
breathings. For practicing the Full Yogic Breath sit in Vajrasana with your arms at
your sides and palms facing up or down. In a single inhalation, draw air into your
lower abdomen, then into your rib cage and finally into the top of your chest. On
exhalation, relax as the air flows out.
7.1.1.2 Benefits of Sectional Breathing
Full Yogic Breathing is a breathing technique that forms a basis to advanced

Pranayama techniques, but it has some important benefits of its own. It maximizes
the intake of oxygen and expel of carbon dioxide. As it involves breathing into all
the regions of lung it relaxes muscular restriction in and around the diaphragm,
ribs, and chest, allowing for them breath to flow freely and naturally at all times.

7.1.1.3 Note
The whole process should be relaxing and comfortable, without any tension in the

face. Roughly gauge the time of your inhalation and exhalation. Take one third of
your inhalation time for adhama ,one third for madhyama and one third for ádya.
Follow the same sequence and timing for exhalation. This exercise can be
performed - lying down in ùavásana or sitting in a cross-legged position with
head, neck and spine erect. All breathings should be performed through the nose
and not through the mouth. As you increase the number of rounds of Full Yogic
Breathing day after day, you will come to develop this practice as an automatic and
normal function of the body.
7.1.2

Bhramari Pranayama

The word ‘bhramari’ (ॅामर) refers to the humming sound made by a ‘bhramara’ (a
bumble bee in Sanskrit). The Bhramari Pranayama consists in making a deep, soft
humming bee-like sound while exhaling. Because Bhramari Pranayama has a very
calming effect on the nerves and the mind, it is usually practiced as a prelude to
meditation. As a part of the pranayama routine, it should be practiced at the end of
other pranayama practices, usually after the alternate nostril breathing (naadi
shuddhi), prior to getting ready for meditation.
Bhramari Pranayama is also practiced as a part of the yoga tradition called Nada
Yoga (yoga of sound). As the name implies, Nada Yoga uses the element of sound,
both external and internal, to reach higher levels of consciousness. In the subtle
51

stage of Nada Yoga, one listens to internal sounds allowing the perceived sound to
arise spontaneously. The Bhramari Pranayama is used as a preliminary stage of the
practice of Nada Yoga.
7.1.2.1 Shanmukhi Mudra
Bhramari Pranayama is practiced while applying the Shanmukhi Mudra (also

known as Yoni Mudra). The word ‘shanmukhi’ (शमुखी) is composed of two Sanskrit
words – ‘shat’ (six) and ‘mukhi’ (mouths or gates). So the full name means the ‘sixgated mudra’. The six gates refer to the two eyes, two ears, nose and the mouth. The
purpose of the Shanmukhi Mudra is to symbolically shut the mind from the five
senses so the mind can maintain an inward focus in the state of pratyahara (sense
withdrawal) and get ready for meditation.
To shut the senses, the thumbs are used to plug the ears, the index fingers are
placed on the eyelids, the middle fingers on the two nostrils, the ring fingers above
the upper lip and the little fingers under the lower lip. There is no effort to press
tightly against these organs but a light pressure is applied with the thumbs and
fingers.
7.1.2.2 Method of doing Bhramari Pranayama
1. Sit in any comfortable, cross-legged meditation posture with the spine

upright, arms and shoulders relaxed.
2. Apply the Shanmukhi Mudra as explained above. Apply light pressure with
