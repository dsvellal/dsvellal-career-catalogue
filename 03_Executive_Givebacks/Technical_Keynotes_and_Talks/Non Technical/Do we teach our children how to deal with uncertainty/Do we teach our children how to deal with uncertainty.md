## Slide 7: How to help children deal with them?

- www.dsvellal.com

> **Notes:** Embrace: Teach children to accept reality, and not dwell in “if this, then that, then this, then what….” Eg: My kids, if a glass of milk spills on the ground, they start crying. What should I do? Tell them to stop crying. Ask them to bring a cloth, clean it up along with them (because we have to teach responsibility for action), and then give them another glass of milk if available. What did we do here? We looked at a situation, instead of feeling sad about it, or worrying about it, let’s look at what action needs to be taken. This helps us to be in the present!

Solution v/s problem: Teach children to focus on solutions instead of problems. A colleague of mine was having a conversation. He said “I barely passed my 1st semester, and I was very scared of showing results to my father. That day, after seeing my result, my father took me to a movie. After the movie he said – it’s ok, you have barely passed this time. Let’s correct this. If you are finding it difficult to study, let me arrange for tuitions. I respected my father very much after that, and I still remember this incident even today”! – what happened here? Father could have reacted in any way, he could have shouted, shamed his son, told him he is studying well and needs to put more attention into studies.. But instead, he focused on reality, and arriving at a solution. This colleague of mine is now one of the highest paid software engineer for his age in India, and is an autodidact, who focusses on solutions and not problems! Such reactions molds a personality.

Introspect: Teach children to introspect. Encourage them to ask the “W” questions (why, what, when, where etc.). Its important to understand why we are facing an uncertainty, and many times, introspecting helps. There are many other methods of doing this including, meditation, talking to a good friend, getting help from a therapist, informal conversations between teacher and student. Adopt an option that works best based on the personality type of the student. The key is to engage them in introspection & not make it a burden. Introspection should become a part of lifestyle.

Explore options: Teach children to keep exploring options. In my case, if I am fired today from a software engineering job – what should I do? Example: I lost my job in 2015 Oct, and I did not have any job till Feb 2016. In this time, I have applied to 99 jobs, attended 32 interviews, failed 26 times and received 6 job offers! I was able to sustain my family without a financial stress, and even today, I continue to plan towards a financial “emergency fund” that I would need in case of a similar incidence. Also, I have a degree in yoga, I go to different colleges and offer my technical/motivational perspectives, I am a mentor to different groups, I babysit kids, I can teach yoga, I volunteer with different organizations – these are all my options.

Selfcare & grow: During uncertainty, it is but natural that a child develops self-doubt and this may lead to low self esteem and low confidence. It’s important to let go of such thoughts and heal. As teachers/mentors/pillars-of-strength we can offer words of comfort. I would like to state an example of one of my mentees I was mentoring via mentortogether.org program. Pradeep was in his final year BE, from NIE mysore. He was going through difficult times (lost his father at a young age, supported by maternal side, has an elder sister of marriageable age, needs to earn to make his living). During our mentorship program, I constantly had conversations with Pradeep, about how he felt, offered him options, told him to give time for himself, focus also on what he likes (in this case, he loved to play piano), sent him a piano that he can play. Being kind to ourselves during this time, allowing us to heal, and utilizing our time to grow really helps! As teachers we should facilitate this.

React vs respond: Teach kids to respond, and not to react. Eg: My kids, we allow them to eat food on their own, and when they do so, food falls on the ground. First few times when this happens, they feel frustrated, they cry, shout, point fingers. It’s important as teachers to show them how to respond. What we did was, we comforted them, and said “It’s ok. Let’s clean up first, and then we can get you some more food”. How has this influenced our kids? – (only sometimes though!) If they spill the food, they say – it’s ok mom, I will clean this – and they clean it, and they come and ask me or my wife – can I have some more please. 

Quarrels – Twins. Who wins? In our case, we let them decide and only interfere if there’s a physical harm being witnessed. Kids nowadays are very mature. They need to be shown directions and they can explore the path by themselves!

Explore v/s guide: One of the observations that I have made is, when I explore something, I learn more than when I am guided. That doesn’t mean that I do not need guidance, I absolutely do, but its also important for me to explore, fail, fall down, learn from my mistakes. As teachers we need to strike a right balance between when to advise the students to explore, vs when to guide them. Different kids have different needs, and it’s always important to understand their needs and then respond accordingly.

Snapshot happiness: Please consider providing this guidance to kids. When we go through a negative emotion it may cause physical and mental harm. When we replay the same incident in our mind, at a later point of time, although no physical harm is caused, the mental harm is same if not more! Reversing this why not replay happy moments? Encourage children to snapshot happy moments and ask them to reflect on these happy moments when they are feeling low or uncertain about themselves. This is a psychological energy booster for them to understand their self-worth and allows them to heal quickly!
