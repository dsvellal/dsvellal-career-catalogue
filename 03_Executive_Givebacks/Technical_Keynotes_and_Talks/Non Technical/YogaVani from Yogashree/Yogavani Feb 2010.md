Pancha Maha Yagna
Bhagini Vanita, Yogashree
Yagna means - ceremonial rites. Sri Krishna has given a lot of importance to Yagna. He wishes the entire life to become a
Yagna. The cycle of nature is pivoted on the concept of Yagna. From the day we were born, we are dependent on nature
in one form or the other and this is helping us grow. Identifying the minute aspects of nature on which we are dependent
on and to pay our respects, our ancestors performed the following Yagnas know as "Pancha Maha Yagna". They are * Brahma Yagna or Rishi Yagna
* Deva Yagna
* Pitru Yagna
* Nara Yagna or Manushya Yagna
* Bhuta Yagna
Brahma Yagna - The Japa, Tapa and Swadhyaya (the holy chants, penance and self study) performed in the morning
constitutes Brahma Yagna or Rishi Yagna. Brahma Yagna salutes the Rishis responsible for providing us with such great
concepts. The Yagna mainly includes reading, reciting and propagating the treasures hidden in the ancient sacred Hindu
texts like the Vedas, Upanishads, Ramayana, Mahabharatha, Bhagavadhgeetha etc.
Deva Yagna - The essence of this Yagna lies in the understanding that the supreme creator has not only created an
environment to live but also provided each one of us with sufficient resources (personal and/or natural) to sustain and
continue life. All these resources can be represented in the form of 5 basic elements - Fire, Air, Space, Land and Water.
In this yagna, we give our respects to the five basic elements.
Pitru Yagna - This yagna brings out the fact that our well being is because of the hard work put in by our parents and
ancestors. This Yagna is performed in two parts. The first one is a Yagna of taking care of our parents and ancestors via
Karma Yoga, and the second part of the Yagna is performed by remembering and respecting our ancestors who are not
with us. This is done by performing rites and rituals once in a year.
Nara Yagna or Manushya Yagna - In a society, an individual is dependent visibly or invisibly on many others.
Remembering this dependency and saluting all the sources responsible for our current position constitutes this Yagna.
This can be performed by helping the poor and needy in what ever small way possible.
Bhuta Yagna - A person in a society is not just supported by the visible and invisible hands talked in Nara Yagna, but is
also supported by the minute insects and creatures of nature. This concept has been delicately interwoven in the Hindu
culture, a simple example being - each of the Hindu diety has a "vahana" (vehicle) which is not a mechanical machine,
but its an animal - Mice is the vehicle of Diety Ganesha, Crow is the vehicle of Deity Shani, Lion is the vehicle of
Goddess Durga. Every living organism in nature is given equal importance in the Hindu culture. Observing this and
paying our respects to each of the creatures of nature constitutes Bhuta Yagna.

Asanas – continued
Parvatasana:
Sit on the ground with your head and spine erect.
Extend your legs forward such that your knees and
heels are placed together. With the help of your hands,
place your right foot on the left thigh and then your left
foot on the right thigh. Try to touch the knees to the
ground and turn the soles of your feet upwards. This is
Padmasana (Lotus Posture). Bring your palms together

overlapping the fingers of your hands. Starting at the
chest level, extend your arms slowly upwards along
the central vertical axis of the body till they are over
your head. Ensure that your arms touch your ears and
are not bent at the elbows. Ascertain that your upper
body is fully but comfortably stretched from the hips
to the fingers. Remain in this final posture breathing
deeply and uniformly with your eyes closed for about
1-2 minutes. Repeat the above steps except that you

place your left foot on the right thigh and then your
right foot on the left thigh.
Benefits of ParvatasanaThe Mountain Posture (Parvatasana) helps in
sublimating the vitality in the performer's body
because of the total stretch of the vertebral region.
Further, the spine is straightened with height gain
observed in children and adolescents. The posture
develops various muscles (biceps, triceps, and
brachioradialis) of the arms. The deep and uniform
breathing in this yoga pose helps the performer freely
expand the lungs and chest. Thus, it benefits those with
respiratory disorders such as asthma.
Gomukhasana:
Sit erect stretching both legs together in front, hands
by the side, palm resting the ground, fingers of the
hands together. Fold right leg at the knee and place it
on the ground by the side of the left buttock. Similarly
bringing the left leg from above the right leg, place it
on the ground by the side of the right buttock. The two
soles will remain out towards left and right side. Place
the palms on the knee one above the other and sit
erect, or place the right palm above the left sole and
left palm above the right sole, so that it will resemble
the shape of a cow's ear. Gaze in front. Practise the
same by changing the position of the legs i.e. by
placing the right knee above the left knee.
Benefits of Gomukhasana:
The practice of this Asana removes pain in hip and
lower extremities. It also helps in making the spine
straight and is very useful in relaxing arthritis and
piles. Gomukhasana gives exercise to the lungs as
well.

Events in February 2010
Shivaratri - 12th Feb 2010 – Maha Shivratri (Night of
Shiva or "Great Night of Shiva") is a festival
celebrated every year on the 13th night/14th day in the
Krishna Paksha of the month of Maagha. At
Yogashree, Shivaratri was celebrated by remembering
the essence of lord Shiva, chanting bhajans about
Shiva. Yogabandhus remembered the greatness of
Lord Shiva by enacting the stories involving Shiva.
Surya Milana – 21st Feb 2010 - Surya Milana is a
monthly event that happens on every third or fourth
Sunday of the month. On this day, many Yogabandhus gather at Yogashree and offer their prayers by
performing 109 SuryaNamaskaras. A constant attempt
is made to remember the qualities of Bhagavan Surya
-Always working towards a better world without rest Karma Yoga, Equality, Ability to sustain life. This day
induces positive thoughts to the mind and physical
benefits to the body keeping both mind and body
healthy and energized for further activities.

Benefits of Amla
Amla is also known as Indian Gooseberry. It has one
of the richest concentrations of natural Vitamin C of
any edible plants on this planet.
Amla has been used as powerful antioxidant agent that
also boosts immunity. Amla restores the vitality and
rejuvenates all bodily systems. It is the best ever herb
for day-to-day use.
The Amla fruit has the same antiscorbutic value as two
oranges. Amla is also effective for respiratory
complaints. The fruit and its sediment with residue
have antioxidant properties as they contain Vitamin C.
Amla is also the best carminative. It is used in
Ayurveda as a heart tonic, aphrodisiac, antipyretic,
anti-diabetic, cerebral and gastrointestinal toner. It
raises the total protein level and helps maintaining
body weight due to its richness in nutrients.

Subhashita for the Month
Pratibodhaviditam matamamritatvam hi vindate|
Atamanaa vindate viryam vidyhyayaa vindate amrutam||

- Mundaka Upanishad

Pratibhodha-viditam = At all levels of One's
consciousness
Matam = (truly) known
Vindate = attains
amritatvam = Immortality (that which is beyond life
and death)
Atamana = by one's self
Viryam = strength
Vidyaya = by knowledge
When one attains Brahman all levels of one's
consciousness, one attains true knowledge and one
goes beyond life and death. By self knowledge one
attains strength; by true knowledge one attains
immortality.
