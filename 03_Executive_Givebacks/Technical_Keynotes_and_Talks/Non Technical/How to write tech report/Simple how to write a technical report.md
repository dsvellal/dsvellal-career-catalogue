## Slide 2: Structure of a technical paper

- Paper Title
- The Abstract
- The Introduction
- Related Work
- The Body
- The Conclusions
- Future Work
- The Acknowledgements
- Citations
- Appendices
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 3: Title

- Titles can be long and descriptive:
- Linear-Time External Multi-pass Sorting with Approximation Guarantees
- or short and sweet:
- Approximate External Sort
- or take the middle road:
- Floosh: A Linear-Time Algorithm for Approximate External Sort
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 4: The Abstract

- State the problem
- Your approach, solution, main contributions of the paper.
- Background and motivation.
- Be factual and comprehensive.
- Abstract should not be repeated later.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 5: The Introduction

- The Introduction is crucially important
- A judge would have made decision based on the introduction
- Stanford InfoLab’s technique:
- What is the problem?
- Why is it interesting and important?
- Why is it hard? (E.g., why do naive approaches fail?)
- Why hasn't it been solved before? (Or, what's wrong with previous proposed solutions? How does mine differ?)
- What are the key components of my approach and results? Also include any specific limitations.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 6: Related Work

- Beginning
- critical to take a strong defensive stance about previous work right away.
- or End?
- If sufficient comparisons require the technical content of the paper.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 7: The Body

- Guideline #1:
- A clear new important technical contribution should have been articulated by the time the reader finishes page 3
- Guideline #2:
- Every section of the paper should tell a story.
- The story should be linear, keeping the reader engaged at every step and looking forward to the next step.
- There should be no significant interruptions -- those can go in the Appendix. Important components are:
- Running Example
- Preliminaries
- Content
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 8: The Body

- Running Example: When possible, use a running example throughout the paper.
- Preliminaries: This section, which follows the Introduction and possibly Related Work and/or Running Example, sets up notation and terminology that is not part of the technical contribution.
- Content: The meat of the paper includes algorithms, system descriptions, new language constructs, analyses, etc. Whenever possible use a "top-down" description: readers should be able to see where the material is going, and they should be able to skip ahead and still get the idea.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 9: The Conclusion

- A short summarizing paragraph will do.
- Under no circumstances should the paragraph simply repeat material from the Abstract or Introduction.
- In some cases it's possible to now make the original claims more concrete
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 10: Future Work

- This material is important - part of the value of a paper is showing how the work sets new research directions.
- E.g.: "We are currently extending the algorithm to... blah blah, and preliminary results are encouraging." This statement serves to mark your territory.
- Conversely, be aware that some researchers look to Future Work sections for research topics.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 11: Acknowledgements

- Don't forget them or you'll have people with hurt feelings. Acknowledge anyone who contributed in any way: through discussions, feedback on drafts, implementation, etc.
- If in doubt about whether to include someone, include them.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 12: Citation

- Spend the effort to make all citations complete and consistent. Do not just copy random inconsistent entries from the web and call it a day.
- Check over your final bibliography carefully and make sure every entry looks right.
- Dattatreya S Vellal


## Slide 13: Appendices

- Appendices should contain detailed proofs and algorithms only. As a rule of thumb:
- Appendices should not contain any material necessary for understanding the contributions of the paper.
- Appendices should contain all material that most readers would not be interested in.
- Dattatreya S Vellal
