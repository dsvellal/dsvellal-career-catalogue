A Little Recognition Goes a Long Way
For a recent Connections question - "My manager recognizes the
achievements of my team." - roughly 80% of TRMS Tech answered "strongly
agree or agree." This high score is great to see, and it reinforces the need within
our teams to celebrate successes.
Recognizing the good work of your direct reports or peers doesn't have to be timeconsuming. Sometimes a simple email or hand-written note can make a huge
impression. Here are other ways to embed a culture of recognition in your team:
Group meetings – Use the first five minutes of every team meeting to
recognize success stories from the past week.
In-the-moment – Say “thank you” immediately if someone has helped you
or went above and beyond to make a difference for the team.
Publically – Ask to include a call out about individuals’ contributions in this
newsletter and/or at an upcoming Tech All Hands meeting.

Spotlight on...
Poornima Srinivasan, Technical
Program Manager in Buyer
Risk, shares her thoughts on trying
new things, Laughing Gas, and the
perils of diving too deep.
What is your role within TRMS Tech?
I am part of the team that owns building the
Unified Evaluation Platform for all kinds of risk
evaluation that we do and want to do. As a TPM, I
help define the “how” for the various projects
related to risk evaluation support for different
Amazon businesses.
What’s a big project that you’re currently working on?
I’m working on a RollingStone project to deprecate the existing Buyer Fraud Service
– which was originally designed to evaluate retail orders for risk of bad debt – and
migrate it to the new Evaluation Platform based on FORTRESS service. This project
is significant because it involves deprecating a 14-year-old monolithic system and
migrating it to a relatively new system like FORTRESS, while enhancing its platform
features, with a deadline of March 2018.
What is your biggest professional challenge?
I have a tendency to focus on the minutest of things – diving too deep – so much
so that I miss looking at the big picture sometimes. This is an aspect I am
consciously working on.
What was the biggest risk you ever took professionally that didn’t go as
intended?
While working on a project involving cross-geographical teams and modules, my
team and I made some design decisions which included a few implicit assumptions
on the integration that were not clarified or reviewed thoroughly with the other
teams. The goal at that point was to meet dates for an upcoming product launch,
but the number of integration issues we faced prevented our project from being
included in the launch. This experience taught me to never compromise on quality
to save time, and insist on the highest standards always.
When was the last time you laughed so hard you cried?

It's TRMS Showtime!
What do you get when you mix
musical chairs, tug-of-war, and a
dramatic blind balloon chase with
a bunch of techies?
TRMS Showtime! It's the new monthly
social event created by our Bangalore
Buyer Risk team - Harsha
Nagesh's organization - to promote
bonding, and to help everyone
recharge. The first event took place on
September 21, and teams participated
for some light-hearted competition, laughs, and refreshments.
"What began as a desire from SDEs to have fun at work, has evolved into a monthly
event," said Dattatreya (Datta) Vellal, SDE, TRMS India Payments. "We loved the
energy and spirit with which people participated in Showtime. Creativity shown in
bending the rules of the games added fun and drama."
This month's TRMS Showtime was organized by Aditya Kapoor’s team. If you're
interested in organizing a Showtime in your office, Aditya’s team recommends
taking the lead. "Our advice is to simply fix a date, book a venue, and just start
planning!" Feel free to contact kapoorak@ for Showtime planning advice.
