Principles of Case-2:

-          In Case-1, you were guided by a mentor and your work was reviewed at the end. We observed the natural struggle to be able to ‘check things automatically’. You overcame this struggle with various degrees of success.

-          In Case-2, you will establish quality parameters and ‘gate’ them using tools. ‘Gating’ means not allowing something to go ahead, if it doesn’t meet your quality parameters.

-          Today you will get comfortable with some tooling and establish gates for yourself, including methods of testing.
Principle of production code: If you don’t know what success means in your context and you cannot constrain it with gates and tests, you have no business developing it yet!

-          Once you have quality parameters set: The earlier you catch an issue (either related to functionality or readability), the cheaper it is to fix. Imagine potential errors that are shown to you while you type the code.

The minimal gates for Case-2 are:

-          Your code shall not have any unhandled compiler warnings or static-code analysis findings

-          Unit tests shall check the expected outcomes, without having to manually check for correctness. They shall run without depending on GUI or Databases or files.

-          Cyclomatic complexity in GUI code or Database-interfacing code shall be minimum (=1). The best way to achieve this is to say what the code should do, before writing it.
