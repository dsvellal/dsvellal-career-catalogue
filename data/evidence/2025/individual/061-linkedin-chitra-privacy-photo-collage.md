## Article Content

### Title
"I did not want to upload my family photos to a random app. So, I designed my own."

### Published
December 9, 2025

### The Problem
Refused to compromise family privacy by uploading photos to commercial cloud services for simple collage creation.

### The Solution: Chitra
A **browser-based photo collage application** emphasizing user control and data protection.
- **Name:** Chitra (Sanskrit for "picture")
- **Architecture:** Local-first, client-side only
- **Privacy:** Zero network calls for image data
- **Export:** 4K capability
- **Repo:** https://github.com/dsvellal/chitra-the-picture-collage-creator

### Four Principles Translated into Architecture

#### 1. Privacy by Design
Local-first architecture using **Canvas API** ensures client-side processing only. Zero network calls for image data. No uploads, no servers touching your photos.

#### 2. Quality Standards
Implements **mutation testing (Stryker)** and strict cyclomatic complexity limits. Prioritizes maintainability over quick delivery — enterprise-grade quality on a personal project.

#### 3. User Experience
Eliminated server latency. Instant processing without upload spinners or external dependencies.

#### 4. AI Orchestration
Positioned AI as an **implementer executing high-level design decisions** rather than driving product direction. AI assists; human decides.

### Core Arguments
> "We no longer have to accept the 'convenience for privacy' trade-off."
> "Digital convenience does not require ethical surrender."
> Individual developers can enforce quality, privacy, and craftsmanship standards without organizational approval.

### Technologies
- Canvas API (client-side image processing)
