We understand how important it is to make a good ﬁrst impression. We all want to show our best
selves in professional settings. In terms of psychometric questionnaires, we are aware that there
are times when the desire to present a positive version of ourselves leads us to answer some
questions in a way that we would like to be, rather than in a way that we actually are. For this
reason, the BeTalent BeTalent Strengths tool has ‘Social Desirability’ and ‘Self Deception’ and
‘Consistency’ checks built into it.
The scores from the Social Desirability and Self Deception scales give an indication of how
accurately you represented yourself whilst answering the questions. A high score on Social
Desirability may mean that you have responded in a way that you felt would be socially
acceptable. If the score is low in Social Desirability, you may have been somewhat tough on
yourself. High scores on the Self Deception scale may suggest you have answered questions in a
way you would like to see yourself rather than how they actually see themselves. A low score in
Self Deception may suggest that you have downplayed your responses. The Consistency scale
shows the extent to which you used similar levels of agreement when answering the questions
within each of the scales. A low score in Consistency may suggest you altered your preference
depending on the situation.

The following section provides your rank ordering of your top seven Strengths as assessed through
the BeTalent Strengths Questionnaire. Please remember that every individual is different and as a

Articulate (How I Interact)
You are an articulate individual and a conﬁdent communicator. You are energised by explaining things
clearly to others and are able to express yourself easily, clearly and succinctly. You strive to develop
clear communication channels and encourage colleagues to be open and direct in all communications.
You articulate concepts in a logical and consistent manner using language easily and ﬂuently. This
gives you strong ability to effectively communicate complex issues with key stakeholders. You often
take the time to fully explain ideas, tasks and actions to others and are able to adapt your
communication style appropriately, depending on the audience, to ensure messages are understood
by all. By this you can articulate beliefs and messages in a way that both inspires and engages an
audience.

Meticulous (How I Deliver)
As a meticulous individual, you are someone who is energised by being accurate. Taking pride in
everything you do, you work consistently to ensure all tasks and projects are delivered to the
highest standard. You like to thoroughly research information before committing to a project and will
rigorously proof both your own work and the work of others, reviewing work in an organised and
methodical way. You are able to give detailed instructions and comprehensive explanations of a goals
and concepts, and avoid shortcuts when it comes to completing projects. Your attention to detail
allows you to effectively identify obstructions to high quality output, taking an exhaustive and
systematic approach to solving problems. You work hard to achieve the high standards you set, and as
a result, you consistently produce high quality and comprehensive work.

Evaluative (How I Deliver)
As a solution focused individual, you are able to identify problems and their causes and are energised
by taking a rigorous and objective approach towards solving them. In doing so, you actively seek out
and gather information from multiple sources to systematically consider all options, allowing you to
deﬁne robust and well thought through solutions. You encourage objective thinking, ensuring that
systematic and fair evaluations are made. Your rigorous approach means you can justify decisions with
objective facts and evidence. Your critically evaluative approach allows you to conﬁdently take on
such problem-solving tasks and you often actively involve yourself in solving problems.

Genuine (How I Lead)
You are a genuine individual who is energised by maintaining your own values and beliefs and
behaving accordingly. When interacting with others you maintain a high standard of conduct,
communicating openly and honestly with others. You are respectful of other individuals and their
values and beliefs. You often encourage others to be open, honest and authentic, praising team
members for their integrity. Driven by your principles, you have a clear rationale for your actions and

team, gaining mutual trust amongst individuals. This also means you openly share your limitations,
trusting others will accept them. Demonstrating the core values at all times, you are genuine and
authentic and recognise the value of an environment where individuals can communicate honestly.

Achiever (How I Lead)
As an achiever you are energised by having a record of success and enjoy accomplishing tasks. Not

organisation to reach its full potential and inspire others to perform at their highest level. You often

aspiration and achievement are applauded. You are able to overcome obstacles as you strive to exceed
those you work with. As a result you are determined and driven towards excellence.

Networker (How I Interact)

your work and others around you, with an ability to identify and prevent feelings negatively impacting

seeking advice from others and positively acting upon any constructive feedback received. By seeking

Possible Overused Strengths
There can be potential pitfalls for overusing your seven most preferred Strengths. These more
preferred Strengths have the potential to be overused when you are either under pressure or are
extremely content and risk becoming complacent. It is important to be aware of the potential for
overusing any Strengths in order to ensure that you can attend to the warning signs.
The characteristics associated with overusing your top seven Strengths are shown below:
Articulate (How I Interact)
People who are Articulate are highly competent and conﬁdent communicators. They translate
messages in a ﬂuent and coherent manner, and can capture the attention of any audience in an
engaging way. Their ability to depict complex concepts into easily digestible information makes them
an asset to any team. At an extreme level the poise and assurance they portray can occasionally run
the risk of being perceived as over conﬁdent. Their desire to streamline information means that they
may over-simplify concepts or take it upon themselves to explain things which are already easily
understood, without giving others the opportunity to fully consider things themselves. Under
pressure, colleagues may see them as acting in a superior way. They may therefore need to ensure
that they still connect with people when delivering a message.

Meticulous (How I Deliver)
People who are Meticulous are detail focused and keen to ensure they get everything right. They
will always produce work that is thorough and accurate. However, when they overuse this Strength,
they become a perfectionist and cannot let go of a piece of work until they believe it is perfect. As a
result, they have a tendency to produce work much slower than their less detail-focused colleagues,
so that in time-critical periods they become inefﬁcient. They would beneﬁt from prioritising pace and
detail on a task-by-task basis, ensuring work that is required quickly is completed by the required
deadline.

Evaluative (How I Deliver)
People who are Evaluative think things through in an extensive, rigorous manner. They are highly
analytical and enjoy working with data, facts and evidence in order to reach grounded, research-based
conclusions. They have a systematic approach when generating solutions, therefore may ﬁnd tasks
requiring intuitive, impulsive thinking more difﬁcult. Subsequently, when this Strength is overused,
they are likely to require support from others when faced with urgent problems in order to resolve
them as expeditiously as possible. They maintain a fair and objective outlook within their role, and
feel uncomfortable in situations that require them to cut corners or do not follow the same principles.

Genuine (How I Lead)
People who are Genuine are highly ethical and consistently demonstrate their core values and beliefs
at work. Their strong values mean that they can become very rigid in their beliefs, leading them to
ﬁnd it difﬁcult to appreciate other points of view. They are so keen to be honest with their
co-workers that they can give away too much information, leading to loss of business deals due to
information leaking, or by sharing concerning/worrying information without reassuring colleagues.
They would beneﬁt from considering how they might be able to remain true to their beliefs, without
forcing them upon others; and balance their desire to be honest with the need to protect others, or
the business.

Achiever (How I Lead)
People who are Achievers are very future focused and like to be challenged. They enjoy working
towards stretching goals and are driven to make the most of their careers. However, in setting
themselves increasingly difﬁcult targets they have the potential to become overstretched. They push
themselves which can lead them to experience high levels of stress and possibly burnout.
Furthermore, their focus on excellence means that they may put unnecessary pressure on
themselves and others. People who overuse this strength would beneﬁt from taking a step back
when multiple opportunities present themselves and prioritising them in terms of long term beneﬁts.
Further to this, they should look for signs of pressure or strain in themselves, and in those around
them.

Networker (How I Interact)
People who are Networkers are driven to build their network for the beneﬁt of their career, role and
organisation. They are highly focused on the beneﬁts of networking, and seek to constantly extend
their contact-base. They are energised by others, and enjoy facilitating new conversations for the
beneﬁt of their contacts or their organisation. They are conﬁdent in new situations, talking to new
people, and are often called upon to generate new business and to attend networking events due to
their assertive approach in these situations. However, on an extreme level their desire to push
themselves forward when trying to build professional friendships can appear forceful and unnatural,
and may taint their reputation among stakeholders and clients. As a result, they would beneﬁt at
times from being less forward in their approach to relationship building, in order to slowly identify
possible connections and opportunities and consequently build rapport.

Self-Aware (How I Deliver)
People who are Self-Aware are highly aware of their emotions and feelings. They are conscious and
mindful of what is happening, and are able to accurately analyse their own emotions and reactions to
situations. As they are highly attentive to their emotions, they can have a tendency to over interpret
situations. They can be highly reﬂective and contemplative and may ruminate over the way they have
behaved in situations, or over-think what they have said to others. They over attribute reasons for a
situation or another’s behaviour to themselves, rather than considering other inﬂuences and causes.
They would beneﬁt from rationalising their own thoughts and from being more practical and
pragmatic in their assessment of situations.

Possible Underused Strengths
While the most important part of this process is to identify your core Strengths, there can be
potential pitfalls of underusing any Strengths. It is important to be aware of the potential for
underusing these Strengths in order to ensure that you can attend to the warning signs.
Your seven least preferred Strengths and the characteristics associated with underusing these
Strengths are as follows:
Initiator (How I Think)
People who underuse this Strength are reﬂective in their approach to tasks, and are inclined towards
‘thinking’ rather than ‘doing’. They take time in planning before implementing action plans; and
consider all of the pros and cons before turning ideas into reality. The down side of this is that they
are often slow to act, and may miss opportunities or critical windows to achieve tasks. People who
underuse this Strength would beneﬁt from speeding up the process between ideas and actions;
allowing themselves to use their ‘gut instincts’ to guide them towards a more proactive approach in
work.

Modest (How I Deliver)
People who underuse this Strength are good at selling themselves. As such, they are able to make
opportunities for themselves and their business. However, too little of this Strength can mean that
they are unable to perceive their own weaknesses and can be boastful. They have an inﬂated view of
their own abilities, leading them to take on tasks beyond their level of capability. They are sometimes
slow to recognise when they have received support from others, instead believing the success of a
task was entirely due to their own input. They would beneﬁt from considering how they can
broadcast their achievements whilst demonstrating appreciation for others’ achievements. Further to
this, they could do more to seek out the views and opinions of others when they are working on a
task which does not play to their genuine strengths.

Energising (How I Lead)
People who underuse this Strength enjoy working at a steady pace to ensure they thoroughly
understand their work and successfully complete their work activities. They are deliberate and
cautious and complete their day-to-day tasks to the required standard. They prefer to work within
their comfort zone and can therefore appear to lack energy and enthusiasm for new activities. They
tend to take a reactive approach to situations, rather than proactive. As a result, they do not actively
seek stretching opportunities to aid progression as they do not place importance on this. They may
ﬁnd working in fast paced or dynamic environments difﬁcult, because they will not be able to work at
a manageable and even pace.

Compassionate (How I Interact)
People who underuse this Strength focus on delivering organisational standards and results. They
focus on the requirements of the organisation, and work to ensure that they deliver the goals and
targets. They work hard to encourage others to demonstrate the same Behaviour, however
consequently may neglect to attend to their individual needs and feelings. Their desire to work in
line with the organisational values and deliver the requirements means that they can miss cues when
an individual is in need of support or when people are struggling, therefore not realising that their
guidance is required. They ask questions outside of the role requirements and avoid personal
questions, preferring to take a professional, business stance. As a result, they can appear ‘cold’ to
others and may ﬁnd it difﬁcult to see situations from other peoples’ perspectives.

Composed (How I Deliver)
People who underuse this Strength are consistent contributors at all times and are able to manage
their own workload. They thrive on consistency and certainty, and are steady and dependable workers.
However, they are easily ﬂustered by difﬁcult situations. They ponder over the problems involved
and become overwhelmed with pressure. They view challenges negatively, rather than seeing the
possible opportunities within those challenges, and ﬁnd it difﬁcult to recover from difﬁcult
experiences, turning events over in their minds and viewing future problems in a negative light. In
times of adversity, they struggle to control their emotions, sometimes lashing out at others.

Risk-Taker (How I Think)
People who underuse this Strength are happiest working in familiar environments. They are content
with the current situation, and do not challenge the status quo. They prefer roles which do not
require them to make challenging decisions. They prefer a safe option, hoping to avoid causing risk to
themselves, the organisation, or others. However, due to their risk-averse nature, they are likely to
miss opportunities for expanding the business into new and unexplored areas. They may ﬁnd
themselves lagging behind the competitors who are willing to take a chance on an emerging market
trend, causing them to lose their competitive edge. Furthermore, they may become lulled into a false
sense of security, failing to make contingency plans for the future; and fail to see the risk inherent in
remaining static. People who underuse this Strength should consider which risks are acceptable to the
business and how these risks could in fact beneﬁt the organisation.

Competitive (How I Think)
People who underuse this Strength are good team players. They are supportive of colleagues,
content in their work, and do not need to compare themselves to the achievements or efforts of
others. However, this can mean that they can be unaware of their competitors’ position in the market
and therefore fail to generate strategies to compete with them. They do not ensure the quality of
their work reaches the standards of others, and they may miss opportunities, allowing others to take
precedence. They would beneﬁt from considering which opportunities are worth pursuing and
ensuring they make their voice heard over others who want the same thing.

Enhancing Your Strengths
Once you have identiﬁed your core Strengths it is important that you continue to develop and
improve these areas so that you do not become complacent. To help with your development and to
enhance your top seven Strengths even further, we have provided you with two actions for each of
your top seven Strengths.

Items

Learning Type - Development Action
Stretch
Take on the task of explaining complex organisational information to others. Try to be as clear as
you can, considering where confusion might arise, and addressing this from the outset. Consider
your audience, and try to make your language as applicable to their level of understanding as
possible. Do not over-simplify for those who will understand the information easily, but do not
make assumptions when communicating with individuals who do not have prior experience of the
information you are sharing.

Articulate

Role Modelling
Observe a colleague who you feel is particularly good at explaining things to others in a clear and
simple way. Pay particular attention to their body language and tone when they communicate with
others. How is this different to you? How do they communicate complex issues and information in
a way that portrays conﬁdence in their ability, and the information they are sharing? Consider how
you can apply their behaviours and phrases in your own communications.
Feedback
Ask a colleague, manager and/or peer to discuss your attention to detail at work. Do you notice
mistakes in your own or others’ work? Are you able to review your work in a methodical way? Do
you take the time to proof your own work rigorously before submitting it? How could you
improve upon your attention to detail? Ask them for constructive feedback and to consider both
strengths and limitations in this area.

Meticulous

Reading
Read 'Read ‘Improve Attention To Detail’ by Chris Denny. Throughout the book consider which
details are particularly important to you in your role. Consider to what extent you are able to use
these strategies effectively. How do you feel you could develop your current work style?
Similarly, consider how you can also encourage those around you to check their work and use the
skills mentioned in the book to be meticulous.
Work Shadowing
Spend a day working closely with a colleague who works with data, or evidence. Observe the way
they evaluate the data/evidence: How do they collate information? How do they evaluate which
evidence is most important? Ask the colleague to explain to you how they use evidence to
formulate plans, or inform decisions. Consider how you could apply their approaches to your own
work.

Evaluative

Mentoring
Ask a respected colleague to mentor you in developing your evaluative skills. Ask them to help
you to devise a strategy to become more evaluative in your daily work and prepare some
solutions to the problems that you typically experience when evaluating information. Consider the
possible outcomes for each solution and seek further insight into how you could be more rigorous
or thorough. Make notes of these and consider integrating them into future solutions.

Learning Type - Development Action
Coaching
Find a trusted person to give you coaching surrounding authenticity in the workplace. In your
sessions discuss how easy, or difﬁcult, you ﬁnd it to engage with others authentically. What might
be the barriers to you appearing authentic at work? Do you ﬁnd it easy to acknowledge your own
limitations; and are you able to share those limitations with others?

Genuine

Brainstorming
Write the headings ‘Genuine’ and ‘Disingenuous’ on a piece of paper. Consider the types of
behaviours which would fall under each heading. Consider which behaviours you are already doing
and if there are any behaviours you should stop doing, and whether there’s any that you are not
currently doing which you could start doing. Seek the input of members of your team to help
identify a full range of behaviours.
Reﬂecting/ Challenging
Take some time to consider your future targets. Consider to what extent they are stretching? Are
they detailed enough? Do they consider both short and long term goals? Are they easily
evaluated? Are you driven to achieve them? Do you act as a role model for others? Do you
structure your actions in a way that enables you to reach your full potential?

Achiever

Project
The next time you run a project, actively think of ways in which you can encourage those around
you to perform at their highest levels. How can you motivate others to achieve more? How can
you create a culture where aspiration and achievement are applauded? Do not shy away from
stretching tasks, but set achievable targets and timescales to succeed. In setting these targets and
being driven to deliver, you can help create a culture of aspiration and achievement.
Brainstorming
Work with a group to brainstorm practical approaches to networking. Encourage them to consider
all of the ways that they might be able to develop networks (online, meetings, events), and the
resources required to create networking opportunities for each of those networking methods.
Challenge them to consider what an effective network looks like and who they ideally would like
to have connections with and why.

Networker

Coaching
Offer to coach someone who struggles with networking and discuss your approach with them.
Discuss why they ﬁnd networking difﬁcult. Encourage them to consider whether their concerns
pertain to face-to-face interactions and which situations or individuals they might feel more
comfortable with; or which elements of social situations they ﬁnd more difﬁcult. Discuss and agree
potential strategies for focusing on their identiﬁed developmental needs and have regular sessions
to assess their development.

Work Shadowing
Spend a day with a colleague who works in a stressful or emotionally charged environment. Ask
them to explain to you throughout the day how they are aware of their own emotions, and how
they deal with these. How does this differ from what you do? Make notes and condense at the
end of the day into a list of aspects to be aware of when considering your actions.

Project
The next time you take on a project that is outside of your comfort zone take time to reﬂect on
how you are performing. Consider what aspects you are doing well and what aspects you could
improve on. Take advantage of any opportunities for you to demonstrate an awareness of your own
limitations and strengths. Ensure you adapt your working style depending on the context, use your
emotional intelligence to judge the situation and formulate a meaningful response.

People who are Articulate are energised by explaining things to others. They simply and

People who are Evaluative are energised by taking a rigorous and objective approach to
tasks. They generate robust and fair evaluations, creating well thought-through solutions to
problems.

People who are Genuine are energised by acting in accordance with their values and beliefs,
maintaining high standards of conduct. They are honest and open, valuing relationships and
integrity.

People who are Achievers are energised by accomplishing tasks with determination and a
drive towards excellence. They build success by being future-focused and challenging
themselves to work harder.

People who are Networkers are energised by meeting new people, building relationships

and comfortable in social situations.

People who are Self-Aware are energised by reﬂecting upon their actions, thoughts and

and generating novel ways of working. Creative and conceptual thinkers, they do not
automatically accept the status quo.

People who are Positive are energised by remaining optimistic and upbeat in the face of
adversity. Conﬁdent in their ability to control events, they focus on achievements and
positive outcomes.

People who are Inspiring are energised by being impactful, inﬂuential and captivating
communicators. They adapt their approach and style, creating strong impressions and

Flexible
People who are Flexible are energised by taking an open and adaptable approach. They
are driven, action-oriented and work positively when faced with change or ambiguity.

Courageous

People who are Courageous are energised by facing risk and uncertainty with bravery and
determination. They overcome barriers and apprehension to get the job done and gain
the conﬁdence of others.

Inﬂuential
People who are Inﬂuential are energised by creating valued connections with others.
They are charismatic and seek to generate conﬁdence in their abilities and judgements.

People who are Future Focused are energised by thinking strategically and articulating a
clear direction for the future. They are goal-oriented and visionary, making conﬁdent
decisions towards a better future.

People who are Harmonisers are energised by uniting people through encouraging
agreement, collaboration and inclusion. They seek to understand the views of others,
creating clarity within groups.

People who are Decisive are energised by making conﬁdent, quick and reasoned
decisions without having all of the facts. They assimilate information effectively to make
defensible judgements.

People who are Striving are energised by seeking opportunities to develop themselves.
They are eager to learn new skills and knowledge, actively seeking variety and feedback
from others.

People who are Fair are energised by treating everyone the same and promoting equal
opportunities. They have a strong personal sense of justice, readily giving others the
beneﬁt of the doubt.

People who are Methodical are energised by taking a systematic and well-organised
approach to tasks. Their ability to plan and think ahead means they are productive,
anticipate problems and meet deadlines.

People who are Responsible are energised by being accountable for their actions and
the actions of their team. They are reliable and amenable, taking responsibility for their
work.

People who are Attentive are energised by being aware of, and valuing, individuality in
others. They are emotionally intelligent, attentive listeners, and actively seek to
understand others' distinct qualities.

People who are Enabling are energised by identifying and developing others' potential.
Supportive mentors, they willingly share their knowledge and experience, setting
expectations and offering feedback.

People who are Self-Conﬁdent are energised by making conﬁdent judgements by
trusting in their own self-worth and ability. They are accepting of challenges and
unlikely to be easily inﬂuenced by others.

People who are Competitive are energised by their desire to be successful, achieve
more than others and improve their position. They are determined, focused and strive
to win.

People who are Risk Takers are energised by, and thrive within, environments of
uncertainty. They actively seek challenges, and test limits by pushing the boundaries,
remaining pragmatic about failure.

People who are Composed are energised by accepting challenges. They remain calm
and determined to achieve under pressure, and recover quickly from difﬁcult
conditions.

People who are Compassionate are energised by being empathic, caring and concerned
with the well-being of others. They gain satisfaction from actively supporting those
around them.
