Summary of what we have worked on today wrt the issues stated below:
- 27369: This JIRA has three asks:
o 27448: This is the additional plan-slicing issue reported. This is not an issue to us because: a) It was
not replicable on the same instance that she reported on, and b) On the reported issue, there’s an
APTC change (of 0.01$) because of which we create an additional slice, which is the way we work.
However, if this is replicable, then the clean-fix to this would be by addressing related issues: 27188
and 27283, flagged during HF3, which would require us about a day’s time for dev, deployment and
sanity testing, and another half a day for our QA to validate this.
o Dis-enroll a particular plan on dates that are not generated by the system: For a policy with 1 plan.
This is not an issue. However, for a policy with more than one enrolled plan, we have shown that this
works, if APTC distribution can be neglected. However, if APTC distribution has to be considered
from the manually-changed-end-date, then it is an unfair ask, where we are asking the system to
react to a manual CRM date-change, and we don’t have that incorporated in our logic. If we have to
support this, then we’d have to come up with a way to respect this by manually clicking a button
after the end-date has been changed in CRM and reacting to it.
o Plan-date-overlap on Medicaid and QHP policies: We are breaking a bunch of rules here, but this
can be achieved manually by following two steps: a) Manually change end-date of a QHP plan, b) via
scripts change the end-date of the QHP’s appropriate-tax-household, and appropriate-subsidy
entries.
o Additional comments from Chirs’ email below: “Note exceptional circumstance impacts start

dates but does not change end dates e.g. disenroll someone in the past. Unfortunately
these defects give SoV a business case to continue to need it.” In our product, we support

-

disenrollment of a plan, or a plan-swap, however, we do not support disenrolling an individual from
a plan, in the past.
27444: This is not an issue and we are working as expected! We have commented on this and assigned back
to the reporter.
27450: This is an ask for backdating a Medicaid plan by backdating application-date and Benefit-effectivedate. There are concerns voiced out where backdating a Medicaid (and QHP) policies may end-up creating
additional slices in QHP policy, when both QHP and Medicaid policies are active, and we have enrolled plans
in both. However, if it’s only a Medicaid policy, then we see no concerns here.
