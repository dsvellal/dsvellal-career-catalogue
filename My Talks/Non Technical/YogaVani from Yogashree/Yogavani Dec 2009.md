Aparigraha
Not to accept things which are not-necessary
Bhagini Vanita, Yogashree
Aparigraha is a quality of a Yogi which allows him not to accept things which are not-necessary. With this quality, the mind remains
balanced in any given situation of life. This also helps in broadening the thinking and growth of personality. An incident which
involves our former prime minister, Shri Lal Bahadhur Shastry depicts this quality very well.
Once, a relative of Shsastryji comes to his house grieving about problems in his life, seeking financial help. Shastryji, moved by this
story expresses his helplessness to financially support the relative. At this time, Shastryji’s wife - Smt. Lalita calls her husband, gives
him Rs. 100 and asks him to give it to their relative. After seeing the relative off, Lalbahadhur Shastry asks his wife how she got so
much money, to which she replies - "I have been saving a small amount from the money you give me for our monthly expenditure".
Listening to this, Lal Bahadhur Shastry immediately writes a letter to the government asking it to reduce his salary by Rs. 10
This shows how sincere and mature our former prime minister - Shri Lal Bahadhur Shastry was. He was not only a responsible person
but also a great Yogi.

Asanas – An Introduction
Practice of yoga helps a person gain good health,
mental peace, emotional equanimity and
intellectual clarity. With a healthy body, clear
mind and pure emotions, the practitioner can learn
to excel in his chosen career.
The Unique Features of Asanas:
 It can be practiced by all
The emphasis is given to precision and
alignment in all postures
The use of self designed props such as
wooden gadgets, belts, ropes helps the
practitioner to achieve perfection in all the
postures.
 The aspect of “sequencing” helps the
practitioner to perform different varieties
of asanas in a sequence.
According to the concept that every limb is the
groundwork of the following ones, Sri
B.K.S.Iyengar consequently treats Yama and
Niyama in "Light on Yoga" as necessary basis of
asana and pranayama: Without firm foundation a
house cannot stand. Without the practice of the
principles of yama and niyama, which lay down
the foundation for building character, there cannot
be an integrated personality. Practice of asanas

without the backing of yama and niyama is mere
acrobatics
Importance of Asanas
Four Asanas are prescribed for the purpose of Japa
and meditation. They are Padmasana, Siddhasana,
Svastikasana and Sukhasana. We must be able to
sit in any one of these four asanas at a stretch for
complete three hours without changing the
posture. Only then will we get Asana-Jaya,
mastery over the asana. Without securing a steady
asana, we cannot further get on well in meditation.
The steadier we are in our Asana, the more we
will be able to concentrate and make our mind
one-pointed. If we can be steady in the posture
even for one hour, we will be able to acquire
Sthira-chitta.
When we sit in the Asana, we have to keep our
head, neck and trunk in one straight line. Stick to
one Asana and make it quite steady and perfect by
repeated attempts. Never change the Asana.
Adhere to one tenaciously to realize the full
benefits of one Asana.
Asana gives Dridhata (strength)
Mudra gives Sthirata (steadiness)
Pratyahara gives Dhairya (boldness)
Pranayama gives Laghima (lightness)
Dhyana gives Pratyakshatva (perception) of Self and

Samadhi gives Kaivalya (isolation) which is verily the
freedom or final beatitude.

The postures are as many in number as there are
number of species of living creatures in this
universe. There are 84 lakhs of Asanas described
by Lord Siva. Among them 84 are the best and
among these, 32 are very useful. There are some
Asanas which can be practiced while standing.
These are Tadasana, Trikonasana, Garudasana,
etc. There are some which can be practised by
sitting, such as Paschimottanasana, Padmasana,
etc. Some Asanas are done while lying down.
These are Uttanapadasana, Pavanamuktasana,
etc. Sirshasana, Vrikshasana, etc., are done with
head downwards and legs upwards.
Now lets look into one of the asana in detail:
Padmasana (Lotus Pose)
Amongst the four poses prescribed for Japa and
Dhyana, Padmasana comes foremost. It is the best
Asana for contemplation. Rishis like Gheranda,
Sandilya, speak very highly of this vital Asana.
This is highly agreeable for householders. Even
ladies can sit in this Asana. Padmasana is suitable
for lean persons and for youths as well.
Sit on the ground by spreading the legs forward.
Then place the right foot on the left thigh and the
left foot on the right thigh. Place the hands on the
knee-joints. You can make a fingerlock and keep
the locked hands over the left ankle. This is very
convenient for some persons. Or you can place the
left hand over the left knee and then place the
right hand over the right knee with the palm facing
upwards and the index finger touching the middle
portion of the thumb (Chinmudra).

Seva Dina is commemorated on late Ajith
Kumar's day of demise (according to Hindu
calendar). On this day, we remember the
ideologies and principles of a missionary who has
dedicated his entire life to the society. With two
gold medals (one each in Mechanical and
Electrical Engg. subjects), Shri Ajith Kumar
dedicated his life to the society, inspiring 1000s of
youths. He was RSS pracharak and was the
founder director of Hindu Seva Pratishthana
(parent organization of Yogashree). He was also a
very great Yogi. On this day we remember and
pay homage to the great soul by trying to inculcate
his principles in our life. In a function organized
by HSP persons from different walks of life, who
have dedicated their life to the society are
identified and felicitated. Seva Dina is
commemorated at Yogashree as well by
organizing a small function.
Surya Milana - 20th December 2009
Surya Milana is a monthly event that happens on
every third or fourth Sunday of the month. On this
day, many Yoga-bandhus gather at Yogashree and
offer their prayers by performing 109 Surya
Namaskaras. A constant attempt is made to
remember the qualities of Bhagavan Surya Always working towards a better world without
rest - Karma Yoga, Equality, Ability to sustain
life. This day induces positive thoughts to the
mind and physical benefits to the body keeping
both mind and body healthy and energized for
further activities.
