The challenge (what was the starting situation?)
What was the initial challenge and the rationale for working on this problem?

Powerful trends are shaping the Healthcare Industry and we are transforming into a Solution-based company.
Digitalization has reached the point in healthcare where value is shifting from stand-alone products to
solutions comprising systems, smart devices, software and services. The future of personalized healthcare is
connected, interoperable and 'smart' and software has a large part to play in the customers' end to end
journey of satisfaction.
Leading Software puts us in the drivers seat of the Digital Transformation at Philips and ensures that we stay
ahead of the curve to become the best software company in Healthcare. Philips is much more a softwareenabled company than most people realize. Around half of our R&D people are working on software and
software has been identified as one of the strategic capabilities for Philips.
Challenges faced in our software population:
• How do we identify and unite our Philips software community?
• How do we develop and support our software leaders to drive this digital transformation?
• How do we create a learning organization and establish a growth mindset?
• How do we stimulate knowledge exchange and collaboration?
Challenges in the attraction and selection of external talent:
• How do we attract the best software talent?
• How do we provide the best experience for them?
• How do we enhance Quality of Hires and minimize wrong hiring decisions (considering both selects
and rejects), standardize the interview process across Philips and promote “Hire for Philips” by
bringing in a neutral third party view to the hiring process?
• How to position Philips as a “Great Place to Interview”, by providing an enhanced candidate
experience and challenge thereby attracting high quality applicants?
• How to reduce the time taken to convey a hire/no-hire decision?
.grow is our global capability building program set up to find answers to all the questions above and to invest in
further developing and building world-class software capabilities in Philips as well as hiring top-notch external
talent.
We need to build the best software capabilities to develop top-notch solutions and drive the digital
transformation at Philips – let’s .grow together!

Sustainment
What process did you improve?

The .grow program improved the approach and thereby the impact of capability building within the company.
Rather than keeping the capability building effort owned and considered as a “HR-only” initiative, .grow looks
at it from a holistic perspective.
A strong cross-functional and cross-organizational collaboration as well as shared ownership accelerated the
capability building journey for software at Philips. Key improvements to traditional capability building efforts
are among others:
• Focus on our software employees as well as external talent attraction and selection in one program
• Early involvement of software population in program design (feedback collection at software
conference; large-scale learning curriculum survey)
• Define program in cross-collaboration with diverse businesses, functions and locations
• Shared ownership of program between HR I2M, SW CoE, SW Leader, Philips University, Talent
Acquisition, Recruitment Marketing
• Agile program setup with work stream leads and independently running projects
• Iterative deployment of initiatives and activation of the respective software community
• Short feedback cycles and frequent measurement of progress and success

The .grow core team consists of 13 members from the HR I2M team, the Software Center of Excellence, Talent
Acquisition and software leaders from different businesses capturing all clusters. The program manager (team
leader) and work stream leads are part of either the HR I2M team or the SW CoE. Software leader are
associated to different work streams of the .grow program and key contributors to these work streams.
(Bi)-weekly .grow stand-ups with the work stream leads, 3-weekly .grow core team meetings and monthly
extended SW CoE meetings contribute to a continuous alignment between the different stakeholders.

The way of working in .grow is based on scrum principles. Work stream leads are part of the weekly .grow
stand-ups where progress is tracked, updates shared as well as issues and blocking factors addressed. The 3weekly .grow core team meetings are an equivalent to sprint reviews and discussions around the strategic
future direction of the program are included in their agenda. During the monthly extended CoE calls key
milestones and achievements were presented and the audience functions as sounding board and feedback
providers. Monthly check-ins with the Learning Ambassadors contributed to the collection of customer
feedback and to keep a strong connection with all businesses and software engineers across the company.

Methodology
What methodology/methodologies did you use? Examples: PRIDE, Daily Management, Value Stream Mapping, Hoshin, Agile, Design
thinking etc.

As described in the governance section, we applied different agile principles throughout the program setup
and way of working. Key aspects of it are:
• Identifying work stream leads and providing them with the authority to operate with full ownership
• Stand-ups, small iterations and frequent strategic reviews to ensure feedback is integrated
continuously
• Program manager acting as product owner
• Customer-first and co-creation: Early involvement of the software population in program design
(feedback collection at software conference; large-scale learning curriculum survey; close interaction
with businesses to ensure customer feedback also through learning ambassadors, regular pulsechecks with reverse mentoring participants)
For the SW Engineering pillar, in Q2 2020 an 11 week co-creation and design thinking workshop was held in
collaboration with Philips University and Philips Design in order to co-create an effective deployment strategy
to encourage self-learning, based on real human insights, leveraging the diversity in our teams. Key
deliverables were captured as the following:
• Identify the most important factors which drive or discourage self-learning behavior
• Frame the challenges to solve through How Might We (HMW) statements
• Generate ideas and select the best ones
• Create an elevator pitch in form of a story board of the best approaches
For the SW Leadership pillar, the PRIDE methodology has been applied in collaboration with business
transformation and multiple workshops were facilitated in co-creation (Q1 2021).
Many aspects of the entrepreneurial project management framework were applied by the program manager
and work stream leads. The team exerted significant discretionary effort and identified personally with the
program success, tackled demanding goals enthusiastically.

Actions / Countermeasures
Highlight the key initiatives / countermeasures that you implemented to get to the desired end state and achieve your goals.
Can you indicate how you came to these countermeasures?

We wanted to ensure that our software population is represented in the program and that we have the
required support and buy-in of the software employees. Therefore, one of the key initiatives /
countermeasures implemented to get to the desired end state is the early and continuous involvement of the
Philips software community in the .grow program design:
• In September 2019 we collected feedback at the software conference in Bangalore on the key needs
of the community
• In January 2020 we conducted a large-scale learning curriculum survey with more than 850 responses
to gather insights and guidance towards the key characteristics and requests of the software
population related to a learning supplier and a learning culture
We wanted to ensure that we act agile and keep flexibility to integrate feedback in the program setup. To
accelerate the collection of valuable feedback and for pulse-checks we made sure to continuously deploy value
to the software population and integrate their feedback in the way forward.

We wanted to ensure that the .grow program is executed in an unbiased and data-driven manner to add most
value to our software population. Therefore, multiple decision moments were set to revise the .grow setup
and review in a data-driven way results of deployed initiatives so far:
• Quarterly review of the Philips Engagement Survey results for software
• Impact survey of the 360 development assessment for software leaders
• Analysis of development goals of software leaders
• Brainstorming sessions at the extended CoE F2F workshops every half a year
• Analysis of O’Reilly uptake
• Reverse mentoring pulse check and feedback collection
We wanted to ensure to look at capability building end to end and don’t keep any blind spots. Therefore, we
teamed-up from the earliest days of .grow with recruitment marketing and talent acquisition to holistically
capture the development of our people as well as the attraction and selection of external talent.
For the talent intake we ensured that there is a uniform process of hiring, communicated via alignment
meetings that happened with global bar-raisers. Also, we:
• Rolled out a feedback form/template so that we collect candidate interview feedback uniformly
• Gathering NPS scores from candidates and hiring-managers to measure the interview experience
• Exchanging feedbacks within the bar-raisers community, sharing techniques, experiences, tips & other
relevant information to promote community knowledge
• Collated the endorsements received from leaders/candidates/managers who have gone through the
bar-raisers program, and presented it to the right audiences
• Created awareness about the program through yammer, and started listening to feedback from other
employees of Philips
